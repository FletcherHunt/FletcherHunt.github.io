Teaching Style

Overview/General Overview

My teaching style is grounded in a strong belief that students learn most effectively through experiential, hands-on engagement. I am passionate about creating opportunities for students to actively participate in their learning, with a focus on developing both practical skills and problem-solving abilities. I place great importance on demonstrations, as they allow students to observe processes directly, ask questions in real time, and gain clarity before applying techniques independently. I also encourage students to take ownership of their learning by engaging in practical tasks that challenge their abilities. Where tasks may appear straightforward, I aim to extend students through enrichment opportunities and differentiated challenges, ensuring that every learner remains engaged, motivated, and supported in their growth.

Technology Mandatory (Years 7 8)

In Technology Mandatory, I see my role as providing students with broad exposure to different areas of design and technology. My teaching style emphasises demonstrations and guided practice, which help students grasp new skills quickly across areas such as food, timber, textiles, and digital technologies. I encourage students to explore and experiment, while also ensuring they understand the importance of safety, problem-solving, and design thinking. By challenging students to extend their projects beyond the basics, I aim to foster curiosity and give them the confidence to engage with more specialised Technology subjects in later years.

Industrial Technology Timber (Years 7 10)

In the junior years, I introduce students to the fundamentals of working with timber through clear demonstrations and guided practice. I believe it is important for students to see processes modelled in person so they can ask questions and build confidence before applying techniques independently. My teaching style places a strong emphasis on learning by doing, which allows students to engage directly with tools, materials, and construction methods. I also encourage students to challenge themselves — if a project feels too simple, I support them in extending their work to develop higher-level skills and deeper problem-solving abilities.

Food Technology (Years 7 10)

When teaching Food Technology, I focus on creating an environment where students learn best through practical experiences. I regularly demonstrate food preparation techniques, giving students the opportunity to observe, ask questions, and gain clarity before applying the skills themselves. I believe that hands-on learning builds both competence and confidence in the kitchen, while also supporting theoretical understanding of nutrition, hygiene, and safe food practices. My approach encourages students to experiment and extend themselves — for example, by adapting recipes or exploring new flavours and techniques — so they feel challenged and inspired to grow as learners.

Computing (Years 7 10)

In Computing, I support students by combining step-by-step demonstrations with opportunities for independent exploration. Many students approach this subject with varying levels of confidence, so I ensure that concepts are modelled clearly before students are asked to apply them in practical tasks such as coding, digital design, or problem-solving exercises. I encourage students to challenge themselves once they have mastered the basics, pushing them to create projects that extend their skills and creativity. By fostering a safe learning space where questions are welcomed, I help students develop resilience, digital literacy, and the confidence to experiment with technology.

Industrial Technology Timber (Years 11 12, HSC)

With senior students, I draw on my teaching style to balance theory with practical application. I use demonstrations to clarify complex processes and then provide opportunities for students to apply these skills independently, with my support available as needed. A key focus of my role is guiding students through their major design projects, where I offer mentorship, feedback, and reassurance while encouraging them to take ownership of their learning. My aim is to extend their skills through experiential learning and critical thinking, so they can refine both the technical and creative aspects of their work. In doing so, I prepare students not only to succeed in their HSC but also to develop the independence and problem-solving skills needed for future pathways in industry or further study.
